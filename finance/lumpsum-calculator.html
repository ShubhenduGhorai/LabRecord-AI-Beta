What is a Lumpsum Investment?

A lumpsum investment means investing a larger amount of money at one time rather than making regular investments. For example, investing ₹1,00,000 into an investment product at once is a lumpsum investment.

How does a Lumpsum Calculator work?

A lumpsum calculator estimates the potential future value of a one-time investment using the initial investment amount, assumed annual return, investment duration and compounding frequency.

The calculation assumes that the specified rate of return remains constant throughout the investment period. Actual investment returns can vary.

Lumpsum Calculator Formula

A = P × (1 + r / n)nt

Where:

  • A = Estimated future value
  • P = Initial investment
  • r = Annual rate of return in decimal form
  • n = Number of compounding periods per year
  • t = Investment period in years

Example of a Lumpsum Investment

Suppose you invest ₹1,00,000 for 10 years and assume an annual return of 12%. If the assumed return were compounded quarterly, the estimated future value would be calculated using the compound interest formula.

Lumpsum vs SIP

A lumpsum investment involves investing money at one time, while a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) involves investing a fixed amount periodically. The two approaches have different cash-flow and market-timing characteristics.

A lumpsum calculator is useful when you already have a specific amount available for investment and want to understand how different assumed returns and investment periods could affect its potential future value.

Factors that affect Lumpsum returns

  • Initial investment amount
  • Expected rate of return
  • Investment duration
  • Compounding frequency
  • Taxes, fees and other applicable costs
  • Actual market performance
