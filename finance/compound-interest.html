What is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the original principal and the interest accumulated during previous periods. Because previously earned interest can itself generate interest, the value of an investment can grow faster over longer periods.

How does a Compound Interest Calculator work?

The calculator uses the initial investment, annual interest rate, investment duration and compounding frequency to estimate the future value of an amount.

The calculation assumes that the specified interest rate remains constant throughout the selected period.

Compound Interest Formula

A = P × (1 + r / n)nt

Where:

  • A = Final amount
  • P = Initial principal
  • r = Annual interest rate in decimal form
  • n = Number of compounding periods per year
  • t = Investment period in years

Example of Compound Interest

Suppose you invest ₹1,00,000 at an assumed annual interest rate of 10% for 10 years with quarterly compounding. The calculator applies the compound interest formula to estimate the final value and the total interest earned.

Why does compounding matter?

The longer money remains invested and continues to compound, the greater the potential effect of earning returns on previously accumulated interest. This is commonly referred to as the compounding effect.

Simple Interest vs Compound Interest

With simple interest, interest is generally calculated only on the original principal. With compound interest, accumulated interest is included when calculating subsequent interest.

Factors that affect compound interest

  • Initial investment amount
  • Interest rate
  • Investment duration
  • Compounding frequency
  • Taxes, fees or other applicable charges
