What is a Fixed Deposit?

A Fixed Deposit (FD) is a financial product where you deposit a specific amount with a bank or financial institution for a predetermined period at an agreed interest rate.

Unlike market-linked investments, the interest rate for a conventional fixed deposit is generally fixed for the selected tenure, subject to the terms and conditions of the institution.

How does an FD Calculator work?

An FD calculator estimates the maturity value of your deposit using the principal amount, annual interest rate, investment period and compounding frequency.

The calculator uses compound interest calculations. The actual maturity amount may differ depending on the bank's specific calculation method, deposit terms, taxes, premature withdrawal rules and other applicable conditions.

FD Calculator Formula

A = P × (1 + r / n)nt

Where:

  • A = Maturity amount
  • P = Principal deposit
  • r = Annual interest rate in decimal form
  • n = Number of compounding periods per year
  • t = Investment period in years

Example of a Fixed Deposit

Suppose you deposit ₹1,00,000 for 5 years at an assumed annual interest rate of 7%, with quarterly compounding. The calculator estimates the maturity amount by applying compound interest throughout the selected period.

Factors that affect FD returns

  • Deposit amount
  • Interest rate
  • Investment tenure
  • Compounding frequency
  • Applicable taxes and rules
  • Premature withdrawal conditions

FD vs Savings Account

A savings account is generally designed for frequent access to money, while a fixed deposit is designed to keep a specific amount invested for a predetermined period. An FD may offer a different interest rate from a savings account, depending on the institution and prevailing rates.

Is an FD completely risk-free?

Fixed deposits are generally considered lower-risk products compared with market-linked investments, but they are not completely free from risk. Investors should consider the financial institution, applicable deposit-insurance limits, withdrawal conditions and other terms before investing.
